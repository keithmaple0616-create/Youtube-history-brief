CHAPTER 01
The Photograph That Deceives
Some photographs arrive already wearing someone else's memory.
An American president lands in China, and the comparison almost writes itself.
The cameras know the ritual: plane, carpet, handshake, flags.
But a familiar image can hide an unfamiliar structure.
For many Americans, there is one obvious template: Richard Nixon in 1972.
Nixon goes to China. A door opens. The world changes.
But in 1972, the photograph had a triangle behind it.
The Soviet Union gave Washington and Beijing a shared strategic shadow.
The opening was not sentimental. It was geometry.
There is no Soviet shadow now.
The bridge itself has become the battlefield.
Trade, technology, and security are no longer separate rooms.
The handshake may rhyme. The structure reversed.
