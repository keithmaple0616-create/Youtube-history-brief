# Content Framework

## Channel Promise

This channel helps North American viewers understand current political and cultural events by placing them inside deeper historical patterns, especially patterns visible in Chinese history and political culture.

## Voice

Preferred voice:

- Clear.
- Serious but not dry.
- Historically literate.
- Calm under political pressure.
- Willing to make an argument.
- Careful about uncertainty.

Avoid:

- Partisan ranting.
- "China vs. the West" superiority framing.
- Academic jargon without explanation.
- Overheated crisis language.
- Forced historical equivalence.

## Recurring Chinese Lenses

Potential recurring concepts:

- Mandate of Heaven.
- Dynastic cycle.
- Centralization vs. local power.
- Scholar-official tradition.
- Legalism and Confucianism.
- Tianxia.
- Borderlands and frontier governance.
- Face and political legitimacy.
- Order vs. freedom.
- Moral politics and political purges.
- State capacity.
- Examination culture and meritocracy.
- Historical memory as political legitimacy.

## Translation Notes

Chinese concepts should not be translated mechanically.

Examples:

- Mandate of Heaven is not simply "divine right."
- Scholar-official is not simply "bureaucrat."
- Tianxia is not simply "empire."
- Face is not simply "reputation."
- Legalism is not simply "authoritarianism."

The tool should explain the nearest Western equivalent, then clarify the difference.

## Good Topic Pattern

A strong topic has:

- A current event people already care about.
- A deeper institutional, cultural, or historical question.
- A historical parallel with real explanatory power.
- A Chinese lens that changes the viewer's understanding.
- A conclusion that is neither obvious nor sensationalist.

## Weak Topic Pattern

A weak topic usually has:

- A hot event with no historical depth.
- A Chinese comparison added only for branding.
- A moral conclusion the audience can predict from the title.
- A forced "America is becoming China" or "Rome is falling" frame.
- Too many historical cases in one video.

## Example Topics

- America's legitimacy crisis and the Mandate of Heaven.
- Populism and the fear of bureaucrats.
- Campus protests and moral politics.
- Border crisis and frontier governance.
- Taiwan as a conflict of historical memories.
- State capacity and why weak governments do not always mean more freedom.
- Meritocracy, examinations, and the DEI backlash.
- Why empires fear their own administrators.

## Standard Video Structure

1. Cold open: a sharp present-day question.
2. Event setup: what happened and why people care.
3. Deeper question: what this event reveals.
4. Historical mirror: one main historical case.
5. Chinese lens: the concept or event that reframes the issue.
6. Limits: where the analogy breaks.
7. Present meaning: what viewers should now see differently.
8. Closing: a memorable final thought and audience question.
