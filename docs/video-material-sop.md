# Video Material SOP

This SOP defines the channel's video material mix. The goal is to avoid polished slide decks while also avoiding AI-generated images that pretend to be evidence.

Core principle:

```text
Real footage is the base.
Archive, documents, maps, and screenshots are evidence.
Graphics explain structure.
AI images create atmosphere only.
Title cards control rhythm.
```

## Common Rules

Material priority:

1. Real footage.
2. Official or public-source video.
3. Historical archive photos.
4. Documents, maps, source screenshots, and data charts.
5. Code-native explanatory graphics.
6. Clearly labeled AI atmosphere or concept images.
7. Pure text title cards.

Allowed sources:

- White House, State Department, Congress, courts, United Nations, and other official channels.
- C-SPAN, public hearings, public speeches, diplomatic footage, and official event video.
- National Archives, Library of Congress, Wikimedia Commons, museum open access collections, and public-domain archives.
- Official PDFs, policy documents, court opinions, statistics, filings, and reports.
- Licensed stock footage when rights are clear.

Do not use:

- AI-generated real politicians in fake news scenes.
- AI-generated fake files, fake screenshots, fake archive photos, or fake war/protest footage.
- Unlicensed television news clips.
- Film or drama clips as historical evidence.
- Unrelated stock footage used only for emotion.
- Long sequences of full-screen text cards.
- A whole video supported mainly by diagrams, charts, and title cards.

## 60-90 Second Review Sample

Purpose:

- Test whether the visual direction feels like a YouTube documentary.
- Test whether voiceover rhythm is supported by the image track.
- Expose PPT risk, fake-evidence risk, and rights risk before final production.

Recommended mix:

| Material | Target | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| Real footage | 45-60% | Documentary base |
| Archive photos / documents / screenshots / maps | 20-30% | Evidence and historical texture |
| Explanatory graphics | 10-20% | Explain one key structure |
| AI atmosphere / concept image | 0-10% | Non-evidence support |
| Title cards | 0-5% | Opening or transition only |

Minimum bar:

- At least 3 real-footage or source-like moments.
- At least 1 archive, document, map, or source evidence moment.
- No more than 1 continuous explanatory-graphic segment.
- No 20-second stretch made only of text cards or diagrams.

Recommended structure:

```text
0-10s: current conflict footage + core question
10-25s: news/official/source material + event setup
25-45s: archive/map/document, introducing the historical echo
45-65s: one explanatory graphic for the structural comparison
65-90s: return to current footage and state the episode promise
```

Review checklist:

- [ ] First 10 seconds use real footage or strong source-like material.
- [ ] The sample looks like documentary, not a slide deck.
- [ ] Evidence visuals have traceable sources.
- [ ] AI images are not used as factual evidence.
- [ ] Diagrams explain one structure only.
- [ ] There is no 20-second PPT stretch.
- [ ] No unlicensed television news material appears.
- [ ] Facts mentioned in voiceover have visual support or source notes.
- [ ] Historical analogy boundaries are visible in the plan.

## 12-15 Minute Episode

Target structure:

```text
current event
-> historical analogy
-> Chinese lens
-> institutional/structural explanation
-> return to the current problem
```

Recommended mix:

| Material | Target | Role |
|---|---:|---|
| Real footage | 40-55% | Main visual base |
| Archive / documents / screenshots / maps | 20-30% | Evidence layer |
| Explanatory graphics | 15-25% | Timeline, map, relationship, cause-effect |
| AI atmosphere / concept image | 0-8% | Mood or metaphor only |
| Title/chapter cards | 3-7% | Rhythm and transitions |

Warning thresholds:

- Diagram/chart above 30%: high PPT risk.
- Evidence/source material below 25%: weak credibility.
- AI images above 10%: fake-evidence risk rises.
- Title cards above 8%: too much lecture rhythm.
- Real footage below 35%: weak documentary feel.

Section rules:

- Opening hook, 0:00-1:00: current footage first; no long history intro, no long diagram.
- Setup, 1:00-3:00: official documents, maps, policy text, institutional footage.
- Historical turn, 3:00-6:00: archive photos, public-domain footage, old maps, historical documents.
- Structural explanation, 6:00-10:00: diagrams and timelines are allowed, but intercut them with source material.
- Modern return, 10:00-13:00: current people, institutions, documents, maps, and data.
- Ending, 13:00-15:00: quiet real images, source-grounded comparison, and one clear final question.

Full episode checklist:

- [ ] Real footage is above 40%.
- [ ] Archive/document/source/map material is above 20%.
- [ ] Diagram/chart is below 30%.
- [ ] AI images are below 10%.
- [ ] Title cards are below 8%.
- [ ] There is no 30-second PPT stretch.
- [ ] Every major factual claim has a source or source-like visual note.
- [ ] Generated images are never evidence.
- [ ] Public-domain and stock rights are tracked.
- [ ] Every 60-90 seconds includes real or source-grounded material.

## Minimum Production Gate

A 60-90 second review sample must pass:

- Real/source-like material at 60% or higher.
- Diagram/chart at 25% or lower.
- AI image at 10% or lower.
- No AI fake evidence.
- No obvious high-rights-risk material.
- First 10 seconds are not a title-card or diagram-only opening.

A full 12-15 minute video must pass:

- Real footage at 40% or higher.
- Evidence/source material at 25% or higher.
- Diagram/chart at 30% or lower.
- AI images at 10% or lower.
- Title cards at 8% or lower.
- All key factual claims have source or rights notes.
